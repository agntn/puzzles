## Who this is

A blockchain author who also runs Chainfrog, per [his GitHub profile](https://github.com/kf106). He writes on [Medium](https://kf106.medium.com/) as kf106. In late 2020 he self-published _Move Over Brokers, Here Comes The Blockchain_. And he hid eight Bitcoin keys in it, which is the part that matters here.

## Why hide keys in a book?

Marketing, he says, and he means it. His [retrospective](https://kf106.medium.com/everyone-loves-a-treasure-hunt-93885ae8d80a) opens with "This is a marketing article". The book got a bold red cover, a punk soundtrack and over two hundred people credited in the preface. Plus a [treasure hunt](/collections/move-over-brokers). Honestly, the hunt is the best marketing idea on that list.

He picked 0.002 BTC per lot, roughly the bitcoin per person. The eight English lots cost him about $270 at the time. Then the Italian edition got four lots of its own, after the price had more than doubled.

## Does he know the answers?

No. "I do not have a record of the private keys for any of the twelve puzzles out there", he wrote in 2025. No notes either. He calls that the beauty of a Bitcoin hunt: once it's out, it belongs to the seekers. Bold move for an author. Every answer the dataset holds came from a reader. Would you trust yourself to forget twelve keys?
