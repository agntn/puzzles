## Who this is

kTimesG is a Bitcointalk handle. That's all the record knows, and it's enough. The account posts in the thread of the [Bitcoin puzzle transaction](/collections/bits). Around this challenge it kept making one point: "It's literally just one addition (sub) and one multiplication in the EC group to switch between any internal subinterval and a sequential interval." That one came [the day after](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1306983.msg64696210#msg64696210), for someone still asking how.

The [80-bit challenge](/collections/80-bit) tested that point for real. 80 unknown bits hidden in a 511-bit number. A public key that existed only in a pending transaction. And 0.005 BTC for whoever could handle both fast enough.

It worked, sort of. The key fell, just slowly. kTimesG's post after the claim reads more disappointed than proud. The spend sat there 39 minutes before anyone replaced it.

Then came the [Phy Challenge](/collections/phy) in June 2026, and it's the opposite game. No range, no race. A whitepaper parody, a Base64 signature and a picture of grey emojis around a gold coin. The signature gives up the prize address, 800,000 sats. The key behind it is still nobody's. Took three days to build, and the cat still got a character out of it. Who's laughing now?

## What the record says

Three facts, three posts by kTimesG. The announcement gives the rules and the one thing asked of the winner. The post after the claim gives the 39 minutes. The third is from the Phy thread, where the cat takes the blame for a missing J. Each fact is one or two sentences of what its post says. Winners and hints belong to the puzzles, so they live on the collection pages.
