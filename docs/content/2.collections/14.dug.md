Dug's lecture slides hid twelve seed words. The [public final-word post](https://njump.me/note157473tjlhl8046c4uhxk6889nwgflwsjtlpwgzfuqp0lmq8vvzas0km4hc) includes the slide with `12. Kingdom`. That post is the official hint source, not another solver's transcription.

## Three targets in one collection

The collection has one puzzle for each funded address: `dug/2025-0`, `dug/2025-1` and `dug/2025-2`. Each uses the [published BIP39 phrase](https://github.com/floflo777/open-crypto-puzzles/tree/main/4-solved/dug-student-treasure-hunt-63ksats) with an empty passphrase and its own BIP84 path, `m/84'/0'/0'/0/i`. The final-word hint belongs to the collection because it applies to all three.

```bash
puzzles verify dug/2025-0
puzzles verify dug/2025-1
puzzles verify dug/2025-2
```

The three prizes total **159,186 satoshis**. The source title's 63,216 satoshis covers only index 1, recovered by floflo777. Indices 0 and 2 had been claimed earlier by someone the source doesn't identify.

The [solution ledger](/assets/dug/2025-solution.md) records all three addresses and both claims. Claim amounts in these records are the inputs spent from each target, before fees. The earlier transaction's joint payout isn't counted twice.
