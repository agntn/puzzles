Twelve seed words hidden across lecture slides, with bitcoin at the end. That's one way to make students read the slides.

Dug's 2025 Student Treasure Hunt has **three funded Bitcoin addresses**, each a puzzle in the `dug` collection. Together they held **159,186 satoshis**. All three prizes have been claimed, and the published seed verifies against every address.

::collection-facts{collection="dug"}
::

## The official Nostr clue says Kingdom

Dug's [October 7 post on Nostr][clue] includes a photo of a lecture slide:

> Twelfth and Final Treasure Hunt Word: 12. Kingdom

One word down. Eleven to go. According to [floflo777's solution][solution], the other words were in private lecture slides, but a public Seed Cipher sheet encoded the same phrase. That gave people outside the class a way in.

The shared hint links to **Dug's own post**, with [his slide photo][slide] as confirmation. The solver's write-up is the source for the recovered phrase, not a substitute for the author's clue.

## BIP39 solution and BIP84 derivation paths

The published twelve-word mnemonic is:

```text
profit general lava hover jar visa joy immense install first give kingdom
```

It's BIP39 with an **empty passphrase**. The three receiving addresses use BIP84 paths `m/84'/0'/0'/0/0`, `m/84'/0'/0'/0/1` and `m/84'/0'/0'/0/2`. Same seed, different final index. Stopping at index 0 would leave two targets unchecked.

Run the verifier for each puzzle:

```bash
puzzles verify dug/2025-0
puzzles verify dug/2025-1
puzzles verify dug/2025-2
```

```text
OK	dug/2025-0
OK	dug/2025-1
OK	dug/2025-2
```

This is a public puzzle seed, not a wallet to reuse. Please don't give the internet free bitcoin.

## Who claimed the three Bitcoin prizes?

| Puzzle       |       Prize | Claim date     | Solver              |
| ------------ | ----------: | -------------- | ------------------- |
| `dug/2025-0` | 75,082 sats | July 8, 2026   | Not identified      |
| `dug/2025-1` | 63,216 sats | August 2, 2026 | [floflo777][solver] |
| `dug/2025-2` | 20,888 sats | July 8, 2026   | Not identified      |

[floflo777][solver], also [0xFlorent_ on Twitter][twitter], describes recovering the second prize in his write-up. Its transaction paid **59,916 sats after fees**. Someone had already claimed indices 0 and 2 in a single transaction a few weeks earlier. The source doesn't name that solver.

So the write-up's “63ksats” title describes one recovery, not the whole collection. The [address and transaction ledger](/assets/dug/2025-solution.md) has the full accounting. In these records, claim amounts mean the inputs spent from each target before fees, not two copies of a shared payout.
