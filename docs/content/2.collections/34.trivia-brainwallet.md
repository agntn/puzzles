November 4, 2017. r/bitcoinpuzzles had been quiet for years, and a user called [Kierkegaard_Soren](https://www.reddit.com/user/Kierkegaard_Soren/) wanted it back. The plan was **0.05 BTC**, about $300. Then Bitcoin went up, so the [post](https://www.reddit.com/r/bitcoinpuzzles/comments/7asy51/brainwallet_puzzle_004_btc_reward/) went live with **0.04 BTC** and twelve riddles. Six hours later the wallet was empty.

## How the trivia brainwallet works

### Ten answers for the passphrase, two for the salt

Every riddle gives one lowercase word or one number. Answers 1 to 10 go into brainwallet.io in order, one space between them. Answers 11 and 12 go into the salt field of the "generic" tab, also one space apart. Hit generate, compare the address with the one in the post. If it's different, the post says, check your spaces first and your answers second.

That's a nice touch, and it's also the only feedback you get. One wrong character anywhere in twelve answers and you see a random address. No partial credit.

### What brainwallet.io actually does with it

The generic tab doesn't hash the passphrase once. It runs scrypt over the passphrase and the salt with `N = 2^18`, `r = 8`, `p = 1` and 32 bytes of output, turns that into 64 hex characters, and hashes those characters with SHA-256. The result is the private key, and the address is the uncompressed P2PKH one.

The site is gone, but its code isn't. The Wayback Machine has [brainwallet.io from October 11, 2017](https://web.archive.org/web/20171011071719/https://brainwallet.io/), 24 days before the launch, and that page is byte for byte the file kept in [routman/brainwallet.io](https://github.com/routman/brainwallet.io) at commit `7c9ca3f`. The detail that matters is the hex step. SHA-256 of the raw scrypt bytes gives a different key, and so does the compressed address. Only the page's own reading lands on the prize.

### Twelve riddles, twelve trips down a rabbit hole

The post promised "vast areas of trivia" and wasn't kidding. A string of hex bytes that decodes to coordinates and the question "what are they selling (hoosier street)". A what3words address. Half a Beyoncé album plus half a Law and Order actor, which makes an Arnold Palmer, whose last US Open round fell on the day of the O.J. Simpson chase, so you add up the freeways from the chase. A patent issued 138 years before the launch day, minus the area code of Dayton, Ohio. A logic puzzle about seven people in a line, spelled out as one 82-digit number. The 1834 chess match between De la Bourdonnais and McDonnell.

Clue 6 is the cheeky one. "Multiply that result by the year I was born." The author's handle is Kierkegaard_Soren, and Søren Kierkegaard was born in 1813. So that's the year.

## Who solved the brainwallet puzzle

### Quantris, with some help from brute force

The claim went into block 493,125 at 02:05:31 UTC on November 5. Eleven seconds after that block's timestamp, [Quantris](/solvers/quantris) wrote "Claimed it :)" in the thread. Half the prize went to charity. The spend sends 0.019935 BTC to `1HB5XMLmzFVj8ALj6mfBsbifRoD4miY36v`, which Quantris named as WikiLeaks' donation address, and the same amount to one more address. That's clue 9 paying out twice.

The author asked straight away how they got number 12, and the honest answer was they didn't. Quantris "totally cheesed" it by trying even numbers in the salt until the address matched. With eleven answers right and the last one a small number, that's a few clicks. The intended route was a Twitter account called 2 hams for $20, which Quantris had seen years before and still didn't connect.

Forty-eight minutes after the claim came the walkthrough, every answer in spoiler tags, and the author thanked them for it. That comment is the source of every `answer` on the record.

### Why the record says solved

A claim plus a published solution is `solved` here, not `claimed`. One funding in, one spend out, **5h 59m** between the post and the claim.

## Where the key on the record comes from

### The thread never printed it

Neither the post nor the comments contain a private key or a WIF. The key on the record is rebuilt: the published answers, lowercased and joined the way the post says, run once through the brainwallet.io recipe above. It lands on `1E3NARnUX25UgMvZd5EZTutD9yVHTVGDNC` on the first try, and the public key it derives is the same one the claim transaction exposed in its input. That's why the record ends with `.derived()`, so `puzzles show` doesn't call the key published.

```ts [src/collections/trivia-brainwallet.ts]
export const triviaBrainwalletRiddles = bitcoinPuzzle({
  id: "trivia-brainwallet",
  address: p2pkh("1E3NARnUX25UgMvZd5EZTutD9yVHTVGDNC", "8f0c21f95e718fbea2a5dfd417fa1338633d03ac"),
  sourceUrl: THREAD,
  startedAt: "2017-11-04 20:05:55",
  preGenesis: true,
  status: Status.Solved,
  pubkey: uncompressed("04e5a18d9449ea5ee3be007dfa5fc7d8...7b92bdf16fc"),
  key: hex("eccf44b2a700e0149d67331f803ca4f68ba5793df7a4fbb75c3f7d0752faffd9")
    .wif("5KcaZnDWjq8r8SjBPPefvGdYicv4aiQ1E7FR4SMa8CVZn6SSfEi")
    .passphrase("furniture mozambique 611 electromagnetic eve 119658 220285 ...")
    .salt("mcdonnell 20")
    .derived(),
  prize: 0.04,
});
```

`verify()` checks the hex against the address. It can't rerun scrypt, so the passphrase and the salt are there for you, not for the check.

### The funding came first, by 94 seconds

The 0.04 BTC confirmed at 20:04:21 UTC and the post went up at 20:05:55. `preGenesis: true` says the address is older than the thread, and `startedAt` is the thread's time, because that's when anyone could start.

The clues sit in the post and the answers in a Reddit comment, so the post, both edits and all 66 comments the 2023 capture shows live in the repo as [an archived reading copy](https://github.com/agntn/puzzles/blob/main/assets/sources/trivia-brainwallet/kierkegaard-soren-2017-11-04.md), checked against that capture.
