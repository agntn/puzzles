On October 16, 2024, [kTimesG](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1306983.msg64639847#msg64639847) announced a side challenge in the big BitcoinTalk puzzle thread: on November 1 at midnight UTC, a P2PKH address holding **0.005 BTC** and "an 80-bits secure private key", plus the range to search. The one thing asked of a winner was to say which method broke it. The announcement also had a line for the people about to argue: "If your first instinct is to yell 'this is not a 80-bits secure private key', please note this competition is NOT for you."

## How the 80-bit challenge works

### 80 bits in the middle of a 511-bit number

The [range post](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1306983.msg64691846#msg64691846) went up at 00:01:53 UTC, and it's not a range from 1 to 2^80:

```text
minKey = 0x659756abf6c17ca70e0000000000000000000140be6ddd93e441f8d4b4a85653b20b4cdcc5c748207a0daa16191d07a425d8080c276f9412472e0429e61bc355
maxKey = 0x659756abf6c17ca70fffffffffffffffffffff40be6ddd93e441f8d4b4a85653b20b4cdcc5c748207a0daa16191d07a425d8080c276f9412472e0429e61bc355
```

Both numbers are 511 bits long and agree everywhere except 80 contiguous bits that start at bit 361. A scalar that size is still a private key, it just wraps around the curve order n. The trick is to move the unknown bits to the bottom: subtract `minKey·G` from the public key, multiply by the inverse of 2^361 mod n, and what's left is an ordinary key between 1 and 2^80 that Kangaroo eats in minutes. The answer, `0x2d56cbf370cbeef9e80a`, goes back the same way, `minKey | (k << 361)`, and mod n that is `b40e7d34…6be5fe56`.

So yes, 80-bit secure. Some of the thread still spent the day arguing it wasn't.

### The public key only lived in the mempool

The address was funded on October 28 with two payments of 0.0025 BTC and had never spent, so there was no public key to search with. The author promised to push a spend 20 to 28 hours after the range post, and the hint said where the difficulty was: "The challenge involves correctly extracting pubkey from the raw TX, otherwise it's a no brainer." The real game was the race: pull the key out of the pending spend, break it, and replace the author's transaction with a higher fee before a block confirms it.

The author pushed right after a block, and the next one took 57 minutes. albert0bsd posted the transformed public key minutes after the spend appeared, and it still took [39 minutes](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1306983.msg64695204#msg64695204) for anyone to replace it, which disappointed kTimesG: "What if this was the real 80 bit puzzle being emptied, you'll never have 40 minutes." Etar's script found the key in 158 seconds and then crashed on the fee.

The replacement confirmed in block 868480 at **00:37:21 UTC** on November 2 and sent 0.00495 BTC to `14q4SoQwENXXzsVT3GMwDrDUGiW5QZeiDg`. The author's own spend never confirmed, so the chain only shows the replacement.

### A 256-bit key and no bits

The key is the reduced one, the 256-bit number that signs for the address. It has no `bits`, and that's on purpose: `bits` has to match the key's own length, and 80 is the width of the range, not the length of the key. The range and the 511-bit form live in the hints, where the author put them.

The range hint carries the answer. Before anyone had the public key, the range post committed to the solve steps with a SHA-256, `a6c39217…f0651db0`. The steps came out after the claim, and the record quotes their last lines: shift 361, the 80-bit key, and the `%` that turns it into `b40e7d34…`.

### Started at the range, not at the funding

`startedAt` is the range post, not the funding four days earlier. The author posted the address on October 31 to prove it wasn't a hoax, but without the range there was nothing to search, and without the pending spend nothing to search with. So `solveTime` is 88,528 seconds, about 24.6 hours, and almost all of it was the wait for the spend.

### A solver with an address and no name

opchecksig [wrote later](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=1306983.msg64697663#msg64697663) that they own the address the prize went to, and that Kangaroo on an RTX 4070 took 28 minutes. Nobody signed anything with that address, so the record keeps the address and leaves the name out.

### Keyed by the author

`ktimesg/80_bit` resolves as `80_bit` or `ktimesg/80_bit`. The collection is named after kTimesG, not after this one challenge, so a later one from the same author gets its own name next to it.
