January 2019. Ten years of Bitcoin. Pascal Boyart, PBoy on his canvases, picks a wall at 156 rue d'Aubervilliers in Paris. He paints Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People again. Only now the crowd wears yellow vests and gas masks.

Somewhere in the paint sits a wallet. On January 7 he [posts the mural](https://www.reddit.com/r/Bitcoin/comments/adc601/street_art_treasure_hunt_with_a_bitcoin_puzzle/) to r/Bitcoin with the address and 0.26 BTC in it. Alistair Milne paid for the prize. The location stays secret. "Not very hard to find", says the author. Six days later the coins are gone.

## How the mural hid twelve seed words

The rule came first, on the [announcement page](https://www.pboy-art.com/single-post/2019/01/06/Fresque-Libert%C3%A9-guidant-le-peuple-2019). To solve it entirely, you have to stand in front of the wall. A photo only gets you part of the way. The author kept repeating that in the comments. "You can solve a part from your desk and other part you must see the wall in real life."

### Seed 12 words, black on black

The first clue is the quietest one. At the far right, black paint on black wall, it says "Seed 12 words". So twelve BIP39 words, in order. But which list?

### Six words in the coloured strokes

The background swirls hide six zones of short coloured strokes. Six base colours, two strokes per letter. The [author's solution](https://www.pboy-art.com/single-post/2019/01/13/Solution-de-l%C3%A9nigme-de-la-fresque-La-Libert%C3%A9-guidant-le-peuple-2019) shows the decoding table, drawn by hand. It gives `banquier usure mensonge peuple combat espoir`. Banker, usury, lie, people, fight, hope. Yellow vest slogans, in BIP39.

### Six words that glow under black light

Behind Marianne the white background carries fluorescent ink. Under UV it reads `ATOUT`, `JPAVFLU`, `Y29uZHVpcmU=`, `dHJpb21waGU=` and `mq+cC6Ax2+8R8LAnEWgQnA==`. Three tricks for six words:

- **Caesar.** `ATOUT` and `JPAVFLU` are shifted 20 and 19 letters. Back they come as `union` and `citoyen`.
- **Base64.** The next two decode to `conduire` and `triomphe`. The easy pair.
- **AES-128.** The last blob needs a key, and the wall doesn't have it. The author emailed `03012009` to whoever found the inscriptions. That's January 3, 2009, the genesis block, day first. It decrypts to `horizon jaune`.

### Why French?

Because the words are French, and BIP39 has a French list. `conduire` and `triomphe` aren't in the English one, so once the Base64 pair came out, the list was settled. The record says so with `.language("french")`. Without it, verification reads `banquier` as an unknown English word and gives up.

## Who solved La Liberté guidant le peuple?

[Antoine Ferron](/solvers/antoine-ferron) and Marina, who goes by marabrito31. They live a few kilometres away, so they went on the first evening. The black on black text was easy. A street lamp's reflection gave away the first fluorescent word, and phone torches did the rest.

Base64 fell immediately. AES needed the key, so they emailed the address painted at the bottom right. Caesar took longer than it should have. They kept testing the first three letters as `ATQ`, and they were `ATO`. Once read right, the keys `g` and `h` gave `union` and `citoyen`.

### Brute force, but a polite one

The colour strokes resisted. On January 9 Antoine came back with a blue LED torch and a lot of photos. Regex over the French list found four words that fit together, one colour pair per letter: `banquier`, `usure`, `mensonge`, `espoir`. Two left, probably six letters each.

That's 499 words and about 250,000 pairs, more if the order is open. So they wrote scanners, sped them up with coincurve to about 75 phrases a second per core, and cut the space by hand. Fifty plausible words, ten plausible orders. A script called Seekplace hit it after roughly an hour, on Sunday January 13. The [claim](https://mempool.space/tx/e467bab977461b61b15d176fd35bdfb8bf2c39ad03fe9fe1072dddc7486b1bf5) confirmed at 12:44:40 UTC. Antoine wrote it all up [for bitcoin.fr](https://bitcoin.fr/recit-de-la-decouverte-des-bitcoins-dans-la-fresque-la-liberte-guidant-le-peuple-2019/) the next day. His tools are on [GitHub](https://github.com/antonio-fr/pboywalltools).

### Is the checksum valid?

Yes. Antoine skipped the BIP39 checksum in his scans, in case the author ignored it too. He didn't. The phrase passes, and `m/44'/0'/0'/0/0` gives `1NqPw…`.

The cipher steps rerun too. `@agntn/ciphers` gives `UNION` and `CITOYEN` from Caesar shifts 6 and 7, and `@agntn/encodings` reads the Base64 pair. AES is AES-128-CBC with `03012009` as ASCII, padded with zero bytes to sixteen. The author never printed an IV. The one that works, `12345678b0z2345n`, comes from the solvers' [decAES.sh](https://github.com/antonio-fr/pboywalltools/blob/52c4707f058c643821125ef55365229a75f431e4/decAES.sh), and it gives `horizon jaune`.

### "That does not work"

The solution page translates the words for English readers: union citizen lead triumph. Somebody in the thread tried `JPAVFLU` plus 19 and got nonsense instead of `CITIZEN`. Of course. The wall says `citoyen`. The author's reply: "it's just a translation of the french words in english". Fair.

## What happened after the claim

The French authorities painted over the mural in February 2019. The address kept getting coins. 1,000 sats in September 2019, gone two days later. In December 2019 someone sent 0.0578 BTC to a key the whole internet had read. It left in the same block, with 0.0031 BTC of that as a fee. A bot, most likely, and a fast one.

In May 2026 another 791 sats arrived. One sweep took them together with the dust on [Natasha Otomoski](/collections/natasha-otomoski) and [TORCHED H34R7S](/collections/coin-artist). Published keys are public property. Don't send anything there.

### What the record carries

The prize is the 0.28916308 BTC the claim spent: about 0.26 BTC on January 6, plus the top-ups during the hunt. The first deposit was 26,300 sats on January 2, and the record calls that one `funding`. Both came before the post, so `preGenesis` is set. `startedAt` is the announcement page, 22:42:07 UTC. Every hint keeps its original language. Antoine is the record's solver. Marina appears by name in his record, because a puzzle holds one solver.

`puzzle.jpg` is the HD photo the author linked in his first comment. Byte for byte, it's what Imgur still serves and what the Wayback Machine caught in February 2019. The Reddit thread is archived in [assets/sources/liberte-guidant-le-peuple](https://github.com/agntn/puzzles/tree/main/assets/sources/liberte-guidant-le-peuple), with its comments.
