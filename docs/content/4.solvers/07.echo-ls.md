## Who this is

echo.ls is a blog. Zden's catalogue links to its Level 5 winner's post. The record uses the site's name for the solver.

[Level 5](/collections/zden/level-5) went up in October 2018. In 2021, Zden said the first image was incomplete. A corrected image followed. The prize moved on September 22, 2026. The write-up appeared five days later.

Level HALV followed on November 19, 2026. With that claim, every record in the Zden collection was solved.

## How it went

Level 5 had 64 rectangles and a small diagram. Its clues were `-1`, `×10` and `64`. The formula takes ten times the side, adds the border, subtracts one, then divides by 64. Zden's hint says to sum the resulting areas in pairs. Each pair gives one byte.

The write-up says experimentation found the formula. GPT-6 Astra helped with quick prototypes. The published explanation makes the steps checkable.

HALV took a different route. Its image hides levels in waveform peaks across eight sections. A circular gradient rotates those sections. Weighting them by Bitcoin's halving subsidies produces the key bytes. The [write-up](https://echo.ls/zden-level-halv/) includes a Python/Pillow solver.

Zden called HALV "way easier than LVL 5". It still took 943 days from the announcement, just seven short of the image's 950 pixel width.

echo.ls worked on HALV with Will Adcox. The post describes a Gemini 4 Neon orchestrator with Hydrogen subagents in Antigravity. The winning run took 9 hours 19 minutes and 103 million tokens. echo.ls reports almost 6 billion tokens across many models on HALV.

## What the record says

The solver party collects facts from both records. Level 5 brings the method and key, the solve date, the AI help and Zden's catalogue link. HALV brings its method and script, plus the collaboration and the winning run.

Want to check the keys? The Level 5 write-up published its key. The repo derived HALV's key from the published recipe. `puzzles verify zden/level-5` and `puzzles verify zden/level-halv` check their addresses.

The HALV write-up calls the explanation echo.ls's main contribution. Fair. Without it the record would hold a claim, a public key and a shrug.
